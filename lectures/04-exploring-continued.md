## Licence considerations
- When you contribute to a project, you typically [agree to the license](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/github-terms/github-terms-of-service#6-contributions-under-repository-license)
- There may even be a [contributor license agreement](https://github.com/microsoft/vscode/wiki/Contributor-License-Agreement)
- Every source has bias! For example, [Codeberg](https://docs.codeberg.org/getting-started/licensing/) vs GitHub
- Mixing licenses can be [tricky](https://github.com/LibreOffice/core/tree/master/sdext/source/pdfimport#structure)

> In general, you should be aware of the license for your project, but most of the time you probably won't be affected by it

<footer>LibreOffice is also a <a href="https://arstechnica.com/information-technology/2010/09/document-foundation-forks-openofficeorg-to-liberate-it-from-oracle/">famous example</a> of a project forking over (among other things) copyright disagreements</footer>

## Evaluating project activity

- Issues (GitHub) or Work Items (GitLab) are "to do" items
- Pull/merge requests are accomplishing the "to dos"
- Consider:
    - Which items get closed and why
    - Which get lots of comments
    - Age of open items
    - Tags
- What are the official communication channels, and how often do people post?

## Roles in FOSS projects
- You are already a **user** — might submit bug report
- This semester, you will become a **contributor**:
    - Reproduce bugs
    - Write code, documentation, translations
    - Someone will review your submission
- **Maintainers** are the project leaders who review and merge PRs, manage releases, etc
- The [bus factor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus_factor) in FOSS is often very low!
