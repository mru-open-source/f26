### Copyright Basics



* **Intellectual Property (IP)**: Typically a creative work, product of one’s mental labour.
* **Copyright**: A type of IP – the creator has the exclusive right to copy, distribute, adapt, display, and perform an original creative work.

  * Code is a type of **Literary Work**, which is copyrighted.



* Copyright’s Economic Rights:

  * Produce, licence, present, publish, or benefit financially from the work.
* Copyright’s Moral Rights:

  * **Paternity**: Claim authorship of the work or remain anonymous
  * **Integrity**: Prevent modification of the work
  * **Right of Association**





* **Licensing**: Economic rights can be transferred to others through licensing (temporary) or assigning (permanent). 
* Moral rights cannot be transferred – they can be waived (author no longer has rights), or bequeathed by the author.
* Copyright protection, by default, lasts 70 years after the author’s death, after which it enters the **Public Domain**.
* Simple facts, ideas, raw data, and some government works are not protected under copyright.





Protected Code Components: Source Code, Object Code, Visual Software Representation

Unprotected Code Components: Underlying Ideas, Logic/Algorithms, Functional Behaviour





### Commercial vs Open Licensing



Commercial/Proprietary:

* More restrictive, related to for-profit IPs.
* When you purchase an app, you are given a license to use it by the company.
* "All Rights Reserved" - the company retains all of copyright 



Open:

* Types:

  * Permissive: Allows broad reuse, modification, and redistribution as long as the original creators are given attribution.
  * Copyleft: Any derivative/modified versions of the work must hold the same licensing agreements (must also be copyleft).



* Common open licenses for code:

  * MIT: Brief and flexible, allows commercial and non-commercial use of code. Permissive (MIT is the "default" permissive license).
  * Apache: Longer, flexible, allows for patents. Permissive.
  * GNU: Even longer, modified code must also have GNU license, allows for patents, allows commercial and non-commercial use of code. Copyleft.
  * Creative Commons: Less common for code, usually for webpages, digital images, videos, etc.



* Software with open licenses: Linux, Git, Blender







### Exceptions to Copyright





Fair Dealing (Canada)/ Fair Use (USA): Under some conditions, parts of a copyrighted work can be copied without paying royalties to the owners. Ex. Review, critique, parody.





### GenAI





As GenAI is a fairly new technology, copyright legislation has not yet caught up with the new conditions it has created.  



For work at MRU, you may use MRU Gemini (WHERE PERMITTED) via your institutional account. Only input materials that you have the right to use.



Be aware that **you might not own or hold copyright of your work** if you **used GenAI** to produce it. Pure LLM output without human contribution is public domain, but enough editing of the output may result in it becoming your work. The line is not currently well-defined.
