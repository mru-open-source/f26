In today's lecture we talked about the funding model where we got introduced to the concept of why foss needs funding. Where we get to explore the idea of open source software(FOSS) used in almost every software system. Projects are free but funding to development, fixing bugs and keeping the software running costs money. This funding can be used to support developers who spend their time maintaining projects. In the Foss project there are many expenses which we explored in class which were servers who host websites and software, domain names needed for project websites, computing resources needed to build and run software. People who developers and maintainers pay a lot. There are many ways FOSS projects can receive funding, including direct donations, corporate sponsorships, government and nonprofit grants, and support agreements. FOSS projects can receive funding from their communities, companies, and other organizations. For example, people can donate money through platforms such as PayPal and Patreon to help support the development and maintenance of open-source software. Companies can provide financial support to open source projects, organizations provide funding to support development and companies pay for additional support or services related to the software. Projects can also explore project funding by running npm-funds in the terminal, where running this comment will show funding information for dependencies used in a Node.js project. It can help developers discover which projects are looking for financial support.
Some examples of FOSS funding models discussed in class include Pygame, which uses Patreon to receive donations from supporters; Arduino, which generates revenue by selling hardware; and F3D, which offers perks to its supporters through its funding arrangements. We also explored how individual developers and maintainers can also receive financial support for their work. Some examples include developer programs, bug bounty programs which give rewards for finding and reporting security vulnerabilities.Corporate sponsorships which companies financially support FOSS projects.Long-term sustainability which funds projects continue operating and improving. and direct donation which is another way FOSS projects and individual developers receive financial support. People who use or appreciate open-source software can contribute money to help maintain and improve the project. Professor Charlotte also explored the internships/opportunities programs that help people contribute to open source projects while gaining experience such as Google Summer of Code (GSoC) which provides opportunities for developers to work on open-source projects with guidance from experienced mentors and outreachy offers internships that allow people to contribute to free and open-source software projects. These were the concepts of the funding model that we talked about in class.
